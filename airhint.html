Growth at AirHint

Growth Marketing Manager · May 2026 to now · Madrid, remote

AirHint predicts whether a flight price is going to rise or fall and tells you when to book. It has 500k+ sessions a month and about 150k subscribers. Before I joined there was an engineering founder and no marketing function. I work directly with the CEO on conversion, experimentation, analytics, the CRM and revenue.

How the product makes money

Revenue comes from Skyscanner affiliate links and display ads. AirHint earns when a visitor clicks through to book, so I measure my work in booking clicks, not sessions. I also work on new revenue lines. Companies regularly asked about using AirHint's predictions through the business page, and those requests were going unanswered. I took over the follow-up, and I am now working on getting the AirHint module embedded on other websites.

Deals page relaunch

I redesigned the deals page. It now shows the cheapest flights from a home airport the visitor selects, with each destination on its own card. The new part is that visitors can run a price prediction directly from a deal, which the old page did not offer.

After launch, conversions from deals page visitors on the prediction page nearly tripled (×2.9), and their share of all conversions went from 3.3% to 7.7%.

Navigation and the How it works page

I proposed a new header structure based on traffic data, including an alerts entry that changes with login state. Signed-in users go to their alerts, and signed-out users get a page explaining what alerts do. After launch, homepage to deals click-through rose 27% while overall homepage conversion held steady (+2.4%).

I also restructured the How it works page. Its traffic doubled once it was in the header, and average engagement time rose 20% after the rebuild.

Experimentation

I set up A/B testing in GrowthBook: metrics, assignment, targeting and decision rules. The first major test was on the prediction result panel. It failed, so I designed a full redesign of the panel as the follow-up test.

One of the tests, with the test and the metrics covered. I choose the guardrail metric before each test launches, because a variant can win on its primary metric while hurting another one that matters more.

AI search traffic

I am in Google Analytics and Search Console most days, breaking traffic down by source, country, device and landing page to see which combinations end in a booking click. The most useful piece so far was an analysis of the site's AI referral traffic, which had grown into a real channel while nobody was looking at it properly.

99.6%of that traffic is ChatGPT, every other assistant together is a rounding error
~4%of sessions come from AI assistants
~15%of conversions come from them, because they land on deep deal pages
  • The countries it arrives from barely overlap with the countries our Google traffic comes from, which makes it a second market rather than a transfer of the first.
  • Almost all of this traffic lands on a static price table and almost none of it reaches the prediction, which is what the product is actually for. That is where the deals page work came from.
  • I wrote a plan for getting AirHint cited by AI tools more often.

CRM and email

I designed a consent-based email programme in Brevo for the 150k subscriber base, with segments and triggers based on each user's own price predictions.

Smaller things I shipped

  • Loading messages while a prediction runs, so the wait explains what is being checked
  • A share button on prediction results
  • AirHint's one-year growth plan for the CEO

Earlier work: search

When I started, I ran the Google Ads search campaigns in English, Spanish and Portuguese against cost per booking click, on a small budget. They are paused now. On SEO, I look at which queries a page can realistically answer and check in Search Console which pages earn clicks once they rank. For the terms we target, AirHint currently comes out as the first result.
